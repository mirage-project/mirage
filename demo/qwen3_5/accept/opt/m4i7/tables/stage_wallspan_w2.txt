READING IT, and the mechanism -- because 'work' goes UP while 'wallspan'
goes DOWN, and a number you cannot explain you cannot trust:

  Flattening multiplies the LIVE task count by NUM_N_BLOCKS (and by 2x
  more on PATH 2's TILE_N=64). Each work item re-pays a fixed prologue --
  the routing gather, the A-tile fetch, the stage-0 B hoist -- so the SUM
  of per-task durations can rise. What falls is the UNION:
  mean concurrency during the stage goes from well under half the machine
  to near-saturation, and the stage's WALLSPAN -- the only part the step
  actually pays -- drops ~1.8-1.9x. The win is WIDTH, not less work. That
  is also why the standalone ferret metric (whole-grid latency) and this
  agree while the per-task sum does not.

  The WIDTH RESIDUAL is what a faster MoE kernel cannot remove. But
  the more decision-relevant number is the stage's SHARE of the step: once
  that is small, further kernel work on this task has a hard ceiling no
  matter how good the kernel gets, and the remaining gap is elsewhere.

CAVEAT ON THE WORK/TASK-COUNT COLUMNS: concurrency.py measures one
steady-window iteration whose bounds it detects per run, and the two arms'
windows do not cover identical fractions of an iteration (visible as arm
A's task count differing between bs1 and bs16 when the graph is fixed).
wall_span/step, mean concurrency and the span ratio are normalised and
robust to that; absolute work_us and task counts are not, and are reported
as mechanism evidence rather than as measurements.
